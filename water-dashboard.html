Water Pollution

What is Water Pollution?

Water is polluted when it contains high levels of harmful substances. These substances include toxic chemicals and microorganisms and are often invisible. They are dangerous for our health and for the environment, meaning the water cannot be safely used or consumed.

Main Sources of Water Pollution

  • Agricultural runoff — fertilisers and pesticides entering waterways
  • Urban stormwater runoff — pollution from roads and cities
  • Industrial discharge — waste chemicals released into water systems

Effects of Water Pollution

Water pollution can cause serious health and environmental problems:

  • Diseases
    • Cholera
    • Hepatitis A
    • Dysentery
    • Diarrhoea
  • Lack of potable water
  • Contamination of the food chain
  • Infant mortality — diarrhoeal diseases linked to poor hygiene cause the death of around 1000 children daily worldwide

Ways to Reduce Water Pollution

  • Proper disposal of household waste such as medicines and chemicals
  • Reducing the use of harmful chemicals in daily life
  • Reducing single-use plastics
  • Avoiding excessive use of fertilisers and pesticides
