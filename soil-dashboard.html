Soil Pollution

What is Soil Pollution?

Soil pollution occurs when harmful substances such as chemicals, waste or heavy metals accumulate in the soil, reducing its quality and making it unsafe for humans, plants and animals. It is a major environmental issue, particularly in urban and industrial areas.

Source: UNEP — Global Assessment of Soil Pollution (click to visit)

Common Causes of Soil Pollution

  • Industrial waste — factories releasing toxic chemicals into the ground
  • Agricultural chemicals — pesticides, herbicides and fertilisers
  • Improper waste disposal — landfill leakage and illegal dumping
  • Oil spills and leaks — from vehicles and machinery
  • Urbanisation — construction and infrastructure development
Source: FAO — Global Soil Partnership (click to visit)

Health Risks

Soil pollution can directly affect human health:

  • Heavy metal exposure — (e.g., lead, mercury) can damage the brain and nervous system
  • Contaminated food — crops grown in polluted soil may carry toxins
  • Skin contact — polluted soil can cause irritation or long-term health issues
  • Water contamination — polluted soil can affect groundwater

Long-term effects:

  • Increased risk of cancer
  • Development issues in children
  • Organ damage (liver, kidneys)
  • Hormonal disruption
Source: UK Government — Land Affected by Contamination (click to visit)

Vulnerable Groups

Some people are more at risk:

  • Children — more sensitive to toxins
  • Pregnant women — risk of developmental harm
  • Farmers and outdoor workers — frequent soil exposure
  • People in industrial / urban areas

Preventative Measures

You can reduce exposure by:

  • Washing fruits and vegetables thoroughly
  • Cleaning surfaces regularly
  • Avoiding direct contact with contaminated soil
  • Using protective gear when gardening
  • Supporting proper waste disposal and recycling
  • Being aware of pollution levels in your area

Why It Matters

Soil pollution not only affects human health but also:

  • Reduces food quality and safety
  • Damages ecosystems
  • Contributes to water pollution
